How the next question is chosen

Independent first-check successes unlock more demanding calculations and reading decisions. Two numeric successes at the current demand usually move the learner on; a numeric success is required, so multiple-choice guesses alone cannot unlock harder work. Accepted answers within the tolerance count as successes but remain separately identified.

A slip brings a fresh-number follow-up. Repeated difficulty brings a relevant prerequisite; earlier evidence stays in the record. Helped answers are recorded, but do not by themselves unlock harder work. Response speed is never used.

Untested available variations and previously difficult ideas return between demanding questions. Coverage is not a claim of mastery. This is a local view of this device, not a live class dashboard or a GCSE grade.

About this activity

40 randomised variations: starting calculations, balances, interest-only questions, decimal rates, long time periods, account comparisons and assumptions. Later questions include original combined scenarios using Foundation percentage skills.

The opening calculator and purchase message is your teacher’s requirement for this activity, not a claim that Eduqas requires you to buy a particular calculator. Use a scientific calculator with a power key. There is no built-in calculator.

The displayed formula matches the supplied reference: P is the principal, r is the percentage rate per period and n is the number of compounding periods. It applies to an unchanged rate with no intervening payments or withdrawals. For changing conditions, apply it to each uninterrupted stage. Every question includes the formula as a practice aid; this app is not a full examination simulation.

Answers are calculated without intermediate rounding. Give money answers to the nearest penny and percentage answers to the accuracy requested. Numeric answers within ±1.00 are accepted, including both boundaries: £1 for money, or one percentage point for percentages. Exact and tolerance-only results remain distinct. This generous classroom rule is not an Eduqas marking rule. Choices always require the correct option.

All questions are newly written. The examples below informed specific structures, not every variation. The bank’s scaffold questions are teaching supports; its changing-rate, payment, fee, split-account, target, reverse and monthly questions are original extensions, not claimed copies of particular Foundation papers. The activity does not require logarithms or finding an unknown compound rate.
